01 — Rink

The sheet decides the style of play

Officially the NHL plays on 200 × 85 feet; the IIHF rulebook allows a sheet 60 m long and 26 to 30 m wide. A narrower rink means less time and less space — more contact, faster transitions.

2026 Milan Cortina note

The Olympic sheet has traditionally been reported at 197 × 98.5 ft. Milan Cortina will be the third Olympics played on NHL-sized ice, after Vancouver 2010 and Beijing 2022 — outlets have described that sheet as 60 m × 26 m (196.85 × 85.3 ft), on the narrow end of the IIHF range. The IIHF rulebook permits 26–30 m, so venue-level figures can differ.

02 — Icing

Two ways to kill a long dump-in

A team ices the puck when it shoots, bats or deflects it from its own half across the opponent's goal line and nobody touches it first. What happens next is where the rulebooks split.

NHL

Hybrid icing

The linesperson judges a race to the face-off dots — not a race to the boards. If the attacking player would reach the imaginary line through the dots first, the icing is waved off and play continues. If the defending player gets there first, icing is called.

  • The whistle does not wait for the puck to be touched.
  • Designed to remove the high-speed collision at the end boards.
  • Still waved off if the goaltender plays the puck or the puck enters the net.
IIHF

Automatic (no-touch) icing

Play is stopped the moment the puck completely crosses the goal line; nobody has to win a race to the puck. The back official signals a possible icing with a fully extended free arm, and the front official confirms it.

  • No contact at the end boards is required.
  • Waved off when the goaltender leaves the crease and moves toward the puck.
  • Icing is not called while a team is shorthanded.

How the call is made

  1. 1

    Release from own half

    Puck is shot or deflected from behind the centre red line.

  2. 2

    Crosses the goal line

    The puck travels the full length of the ice without being touched.

  3. 3

    Exemption check

    Shorthanded team? Goaltender played it? Puck going in? Any yes waves it off.

  4. 4

    Race or whistle

    NHL: hybrid judgement at the dots. IIHF: automatic whistle.

  5. 5

    Consequence

    Face-off back in the offending team's defensive zone, and no line change.

03 — Offside

Shared principle, tightening wording

Both rulebooks judge offside on skate position, not the stick. An attacker is offside when both skates have fully crossed the blue line into the attacking zone before the puck does.

Delayed offside

The linesperson raises the arm but does not whistle. Play stops only if an attacking player touches the puck or checks an opponent. If every attacker clears the zone — a tag-up — the offside is wiped out and play continues.

Intentional offside

When the linesperson judges the entry was deliberate rather than a line-contested entry, the face-off moves back to the offending team's defensive zone instead of the neutral zone.

Goal after an offside

A goal scored on a play that was offside cannot stand. This is one of the situations a coach can ask the officials to review, and the deciding standard is conclusive evidence.

  1. 2025-26

    NHL wording clarified

    The definition of a skate is tied to the skate blade, and the requirement to control the puck with the stick at the blue line was made explicit.

  2. 2026-27

    IIHF: possession and control

    An attacking player entering the zone before the puck must have possession and control of the puck with the stick to avoid being ruled offside.

  3. 2026-27

    IIHF: high-sticking tied to shoulder height

    For contact to count as high-sticking, the opponent's shoulders must be at waist level or higher — a clarification aimed at players bent low when a stick rides above the shoulders.

04 — Penalties

Where the two books diverge most

Fighting, checks to the head, goaltender interference and goaltender puck handling are the classic fault lines between the NHL and international hockey.

Fighting

NHL

A five-minute major. Matching majors cancel out and teams stay at even strength. The instigator takes an additional two minutes.

IIHF

A major plus a game misconduct or a match penalty. Removing a jersey or helmet to fight carries an additional penalty. In practice fighting is not tolerated.

Checks to the head

NHL

Discretionary: a minor, major, game misconduct or match penalty depending on severity and circumstances.

IIHF

A minor or major must be accompanied by a misconduct (10 minutes) or a game misconduct.

Goaltender interference

NHL

Play is not stopped simply because an attacker is in the crease. A goal can be disallowed afterwards, and the review is famously debated.

IIHF

An attacking player in the crease stops play immediately, with the face-off in the neutral zone.

Trapezoid rule

NHL

The goaltender may handle the puck behind the net only inside the trapezoid. Playing it outside is a delay-of-game penalty.

IIHF

No trapezoid. The goaltender may play the puck anywhere behind the goal line.

Roster size

NHL 18 skaters and 2 goaltenders — 20 dressed per game, drawn from a larger active roster.

IIHF 20 skaters and 2 goaltenders — 22 dressed.

Helmets and visors

NHL A player who loses a helmet may retrieve it and continue; visors are mandatory only for players below the experience threshold.

IIHF A player who loses a helmet must leave the ice immediately; visors are mandatory for all players.

Face-off violations

NHL The offending player is replaced from the face-off, except after an icing.

IIHF From 2026-27 the offending centre is removed and replaced; the first violation after an icing is a warning instead.

Penalty shot taker

NHL The player who was fouled takes the shot, unless injured or ejected.

IIHF Any player on the team may be designated to take it.

05 — Overtime & Shootout

Five minutes, ten minutes, or all night

The NHL regular season runs one short overtime and then a shootout. The playoffs never shoot out. The IIHF changes the format by tournament round.

NHL · Regular season

5 min · 3-on-3

Sudden-death 3-on-3. If nobody scores, a shootout follows: three shooters per team, then sudden death. A shooter cannot go again until everyone has shot.

NHL · Playoffs

20 min · 5-on-5

Sudden-death 5-on-5 periods of 20 minutes, repeated as needed. No shootouts, no ties.

IIHF · Preliminary round

5 min · 3-on-3

Same five-minute 3-on-3 as the NHL regular season, but the shootout starts with five shooters per team.

IIHF · Quarter-finals & semi-finals

10 min · 3-on-3

A ten-minute 3-on-3 overtime, then a five-shooter shootout if the game is still level.

IIHF · Gold & bronze medal games

20 min · 3-on-3

Twenty-minute 3-on-3 periods with an intermission between. No shootout — a champion is decided in open play.

Both books

Intermission clock

NHL intermissions are 18 minutes; Olympic and IIHF intermissions are 15 minutes. Both play three 20-minute periods.

06 — 2026-27 Changes

What is new in both rulebooks

The NHL changes come from the 2026-27 rulebook and the new CBA as reported by Scouting the Refs and Bleacher Report. The IIHF changes come from the 2026-27 rulebook as summarised by the Elite League.

NHL

2026-27

01

Neck protection required for new players

Any player with zero previous NHL games — goaltenders included — must wear cut-resistant neck protection, and it applies in pregame warm-ups too. The rule is not retroactive: one NHL game before 2026-27 and the protection stays optional.

02

Regular season grows to 84 games

Up from 82, the first time since 1993-94. Every team plays four games against each divisional opponent, two home and two away. Preseason is capped at four games, with players holding 100+ career NHL games limited to two.

03

Emergency backup goaltender redefined

The on-call local EBUG is gone. Teams must designate a dedicated EBUG who travels and practises with the club: no NHL game under a Standard Player Contract, fewer than 80 professional games at any level, and no professional hockey in the previous three seasons. If activated, the EBUG signs an amateur or professional tryout and does not count against the active roster.

04

Uniform enforcement

Vision on equipment: helmets in warm-ups, properly worn visors and jerseys, and the jersey-tuck interpretation are all part of the season's enforcement push.

05

Coach's challenge can remove a puck-over-glass penalty

A challenge may now be used to take down a delay-of-game penalty for shooting the puck over the glass, but only where the puck was deflected.

06

Playoff salary-cap compliance

Lineups dressed in the Stanley Cup Playoffs must comply with the salary cap, closing the long-term injured reserve route to an over-the-cap playoff roster.

07

Contract term and bonus limits

Maximum term drops to seven years to re-sign with the current team and six years for unrestricted free agents. Signing bonuses are capped at 60% of total value, salary deferral is prohibited on new deals, and the front-loading gap between adjacent years cannot exceed 20% of first-year compensation (down from 25%).

IIHF

2026-27

01

Face-off violation: centre is replaced

Under Rule 76, the centre of the offending team is removed from the face-off and replaced. The exception is a face-off following an icing, where the first violation draws a warning instead.

02

Offside needs possession and control

An attacking player who enters the attacking zone before the puck must have possession and control of the puck with the stick to stay onside.

03

High-sticking tied to shoulder height

Contact counts as high-sticking only where the opponent's shoulders are at waist level or higher — a clarification for players bent over when a stick rides above the shoulders.

04

Goaltender penalties served by a skater

When a goaltender is penalised, a skater who was on the ice at the time serves the penalty.

05

Coach's challenge in every game

All Rule 38.2 situations become available in every regular-season game: goaltender interference, an offside leading to a goal, and a missed stoppage such as a hand pass, a high stick or the puck leaving the playing surface. An unsuccessful challenge costs two minutes for delaying the game, rising to four on the next miss.

06

Puck-over-glass challenge is one-way

A delay-of-game call for shooting the puck directly out of play can only be challenged once the penalty has been called on the ice. The opposing coach cannot challenge in order to have a penalty assessed.
